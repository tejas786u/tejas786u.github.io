Privacy Policy

C Programming apps · Last updated: 18 November 2026

This policy explains what information the C Programming learning apps collect and why. It covers:

  • C Programming – Learn Code for iPhone and iPad (App Store)
  • C Programming for Android (Google Play, package com.balarnbalar.cprogramming)

"We" and "us" mean the developer of these apps.

In short
  • No account, no sign-in. We never ask for your name, email or contacts.
  • Your bookmarks, lesson progress and quiz scores stay on your device.
  • The apps show ads from Google AdMob and use Google Firebase to count app usage and report crashes. These services collect some device and usage data, described below.
  • Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA, UK and Switzerland), we ask for your consent before ads use personal data. You can change your choice at any time in the app.

1. Information stored only on your device

To remember your learning, the apps store the following locally on your device: lessons you completed, programs you bookmarked, quiz results, and display settings such as code text size. This data is not sent to us or to anyone else. It is deleted when you delete the app.

2. Information collected by third-party services

The apps use these services from Google. Each collects information under its own privacy policy:

ServicePurposeInformation it may collect
Google AdMob Showing banner and full-screen ads, which keep the apps free; measuring ads; preventing fraud Device and app information (model, OS version, language), IP address, ad interactions, and on Android the advertising ID. On iPhone and iPad the apps do not request tracking permission, so Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) is not used.
Google User Messaging Platform Asking for and storing your privacy and consent choices Your consent choices and approximate region
Firebase Analytics Understanding how the apps are used (for example which lessons are opened and how quizzes go), so we can improve them App usage events, an app-instance identifier, device and OS information, approximate location derived from IP address
Firebase Crashlytics Finding and fixing crashes Crash reports and diagnostic data: device state, OS version and a crash identifier

Read more in Google's policies: Google Privacy Policy, how Google uses information from apps that use its services, Firebase privacy.

We don't sell personal information, and we don't use the information above to identify you.

3. Ads and your choices

  • Consent: if you are in a region where consent is required, the app asks before showing personalised ads. To change your choice later, open the app's More menu (⋯) and choose Privacy Settings.
  • Android: you can delete or reset your advertising ID in Settings → Privacy → Ads (the name varies by device).
  • iPhone and iPad: the apps don't ask for permission to track you. You can also turn off Personalized Ads for Apple's own ads in Settings → Privacy & Security.

4. Children

The apps teach programming and are suitable for general audiences, but they are not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the apps, contact us and we will help remove it.

5. Data retention and security

Information on your device stays until you delete the app. Google keeps information collected by AdMob and Firebase for the periods described in its policies; we keep Firebase Analytics data under Google's default retention settings. Data sent to these services is encrypted in transit.

6. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA/CPRA in California), you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal information, to object to or restrict its use, and to withdraw consent. Because we don't keep personal information on our own servers, most requests are best handled through the consent settings above or with Google directly. You can also contact us and we'll help.

7. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the apps change. The date at the top shows the latest version. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app's release notes.
